I have always believed that wellbeing is not something we find once and then keep forever. It is something we create, day by day, through the choices we make and the way we live.

The interesting thing is that many of the things that help us to live a healthy and happy life are remarkably simple.

And yet, somehow, the simple things can be the hardest things to do consistently.

They require something I call **loving discipline**.

Not punishment. Not perfection. Not forcing ourselves to become someone we are not.

Loving discipline means caring enough about ourselves to keep choosing the things that support our wellbeing — even on the days when we don’t particularly feel like doing them.

One of the most remarkable examples of this comes from the Harvard Study of Adult Development, one of the world’s longest-running studies of adult life. Begun in 1938, the research has followed people and their families for more than eight decades.

Its clearest message is surprisingly simple:

> Good relationships help keep us happier and healthier.

People who are more socially connected to family, friends and community tend to be happier, physically healthier and to live longer. The quality of our relationships matters enormously — not simply how many people we know, but whether we feel that we have people we can depend upon and share our lives with. ([Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2017/04/over-nearly-80-years-harvard-study-has-been-showing-how-to-live-a-healthy-and-happy-life/))

This makes me think about wellbeing as something much broader than simply avoiding illness.

For me, it means taking care of the whole person.

## Eat food that nourishes you

Our bodies need nourishment, not simply calories.

Eating predominantly whole, nourishing foods gives the body the raw materials it needs to function, repair and thrive. This doesn’t mean that we have to eat perfectly. It means developing a relationship with food that supports rather than undermines our health.

## Sleep enough

Sleep is not a luxury or something we should sacrifice whenever life becomes busy.

It is one of the fundamental ways our bodies and minds restore themselves.

Sometimes taking care of ourselves means doing less — and going to bed.

## Move your body

Our bodies are designed to move.

Exercise doesn’t have to mean going to a gym or following a complicated programme. Walking, swimming, dancing, stretching, gardening, cycling or simply moving regularly throughout the day can all be part of an active life.

The important thing is to keep the body alive, mobile and engaged.

## Stay connected

We are not designed to live in isolation.

Family, friendship, community, belonging and meaningful human connection are not extras added to a healthy life. They are part of a healthy life.

The Harvard research has made this particularly clear: relationships are deeply connected with both our physical and psychological wellbeing. ([Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2017/04/over-nearly-80-years-harvard-study-has-been-showing-how-to-live-a-healthy-and-happy-life/))

## Have something larger than yourself

For some people this is spirituality. For others it may be religion, nature, meditation, service, creativity, humanity or a sense of purpose.

Whatever form it takes, I believe we benefit from feeling connected to something larger than our individual self.

A life needs meaning as well as comfort.

## Protect your mind and body from what harms them

This includes being conscious of alcohol, smoking and drugs, as well as other behaviours that repeatedly undermine our wellbeing.

It is not about judgement. It is about awareness.

We only have one body and one mind with which to experience this life.

## And perhaps most importantly — breathe

Our breath is with us from the moment we are born until the moment we die.

It changes when we are frightened, excited, angry, relaxed or peaceful. And unlike many of the body’s automatic processes, we can consciously influence it.

This is one of the reasons I believe the breath is such an important bridge between the body and the mind.

---

For me, wellbeing is therefore not about chasing some perfect state of happiness.

It is about creating the conditions in which we are more likely to feel alive, connected, grounded and capable of meeting life as it comes.

The ingredients are simple.

> Eat well. Sleep enough. Move your body. Stay connected. Find meaning. Breathe. Protect yourself from what harms you.

Simple — but not always easy.

That is where loving discipline comes in.

Because taking care of ourselves is, ultimately, an act of love.
